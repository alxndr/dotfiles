# Alexander Quine — Writing Voice Profile

Use this file when drafting cover letters, short-answer application fields, LinkedIn messages, or any other public-facing copy. The goal is to match Alexander's actual voice, not default AI prose patterns.

Derived from analysis of blog posts at ~/workspace/blog.

---

## Sentence structure

- **Short sentences are the norm.** Consecutive fragments are fine.
- **Lead with the punchline.** Answer first, explanation after. No preamble about what's coming.
- **Ellipsis as a trail-off device.** Indicates informality and continuing thought.
- **No long compound sentences.** Rhythm: short statement → brief explanation → example → short closer. Not: "While X is true, it should be noted that Y, and furthermore Z."
- **Lists over prose.** When there are multiple points, bullet or number them.

## Tone and register

- **Casual and direct.** No "Furthermore" or "In conclusion."
- **Dry humor, not performed enthusiasm.** Opinions stated lightly, not performed.
- **Self-aware.** Comfortable acknowledging unfamiliarity or a past mistake.
- **Exclamation marks are earned.** Used for genuine celebration or real emphasis, not filler.
- **Conversational reader address.** "This ever happen to you?" — not marketing, just direct.

## Characteristic constructions

- Parenthetical asides mid-sentence.
- TLDR or result before explanation — the commands before the prose, the answer before the reasoning.
- Cross-links embedded in specific words, never "click here."
- Blockquotes for external text, then own commentary after.
- **No adjectives-as-nouns.** Don't use an adjective as a stand-in for a noun phrase. Wrong: "Legal is one of the few domains..." Right: "Legal work is..." / "The legal industry..." / "Legal services..." — always create a real noun rather than abbreviating it into a bare adjective.


## Opening strategies

- Jump in. No preamble about what the piece will cover.
- Lead with the answer or the key fact.
- Short punchy statement.
- **Never:** "In this post, I'll walk you through...", "Have you ever wondered...", "Today we're going to explore..."

## Closing strategies

- End on the last substantive point or a brief forward-looking one-liner.
- **Never:** restate what was just covered, say "I hope this was helpful!", summarize conclusions.

## Application-specific register

Job application copy uses complete sentences and slightly more formal register than the blog — but the core traits hold: direct, specific, no padding, no performative hedging. Avoid generic openers like "I am a passionate engineer who..." or closers like "I look forward to the opportunity to..." — neither sounds like him.

**Do not assume preferences, habits, or hobbies.** If there's a personal angle that could strengthen a draft (e.g. "I'm a live music fan," "I care about climate," "I use this product"), ask Alexander first rather than inventing it. He will confirm, correct, or decline — and the draft will be more accurate for it.
